# Participant group interview 2

- **Basis:** real conversations, anonymised composite
- **Format:** group interview, excerpt
- **Date:** Early summer
- **Participants:** P1, product owner · P2, product manager on the business side · P3, product owner · P4, member of a product team
- **Interviewer:** I

---

**I:** Let's start with how you came into the format. What did you expect?

**P1:** Honestly, I didn't really know what to expect. I first had to find out what the assistant even is and what it can do. My expectation was more along the lines of: I'll have an idea of what I could do with it. Not: this is what I'm going to do with it.

**P3:** I walked in on the first morning thinking, what am I doing here. I had zero clue and I thought it would be far too much to get into.

**P4:** It was different for me. I already have several agents running in parallel and I mostly work at the level of the spec. I'd connected tools through a connector before, written session instructions, written a skill.

**I:** So quite a range in one room.

**P3:** Yes. And following someone else's screen while trying to do something on my own at the same time, that was hard. Remote makes that parallel thing even harder. I didn't manage it well.

**I:** P2, you've done trainings before. How did those go?

**P2:** I had two, a basic one and an advanced one, both on AI in product management. The things in there I then just did myself with the assistant. In conversation, basically. In the advanced one they used tools we simply can't use here. For some of the use cases that was a bit silly, because I couldn't implement them at all.

**I:** Is tool access the main thing that gets in the way?

**P1:** For me it was hardware first. I had to request more memory for my laptop so this runs at all. Once that's there I can keep working on my skill.

**P2:** With data it's a topic too. Finding the right sources, the permissions and so on. That's sometimes a thing.

**P4:** I'd already connected tools through a connector before the format started, so that part was in place.

**I:** P1, what did you try on your own?

**P1:** Before the format I'd already tried something. I want to break down concepts and tickets faster. I connected it to our ticket and wiki tools, and that was a big aha moment for me. I know I can use it a lot better once it's set up properly. I'm looking forward to that.

**I:** And now, after the format?

**P3:** I felt very encouraged to actually try things myself. The explanations helped me build a base where I can experiment with a clear conscience. I'll be able to get a few things going with it.

**P4:** What I wanted to leave with was an idea of how to apply this, or part of it, to actual features on our roadmap. A direction for how to use it on a daily basis. And a plan for how we move forward in the next weeks, with next steps.

**I:** Did you get that?

**P4:** The first day I was a bit lost at the start. No schedule, no concrete goal for the day. That was the biggest gap.

**I:** P2, where do you stand?

**P2:** I don't feel that advanced, to be honest. Some of the bigger topics, I lack a bit of imagination for how I would use it, what it would look like, what it can all do.

**I:** What about working together with others, beyond your own use?

**P4:** What I hoped for was a shared picture of how we use AI collectively, not each person one to one with their assistant. An agreed way of working on product features. And some guidance on a shared setup where we learn from each other.

**P1:** I still need the material afterwards to reread. When you actually sit down to do it, you need it again as a memory aid.

**I:** Anything else you'd want me to take away?

**P3:** Maybe a small shared project when it's remote, so everyone can work on it and nobody loses the thread. In our group it was okay. Everyone took a lot away.

**I:** Thank you.
