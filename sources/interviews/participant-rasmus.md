# Participant interview: Rasmus, project and requirements lead, grocery retailer

- **Basis:** synthetic, built from patterns in real conversations
- **Format:** one-on-one interview, full conversation
- **Date:** Autumn
- **Interviewee:** Rasmus Kühn (he/him), project and requirements lead in a product team at a grocery retailer that is rebuilding its customer-facing ordering system; no line responsibility; interviewed before any booking, at the request of the sponsor at his company
- **Interviewer:** Loopwise

---

**Rasmus:** Hello, sorry, I'm a minute late. Refinement. Not because it dragged on, for once it finished early, which never happens, so I stayed to finish a couple of things while everyone was still in a good mood.

**Interviewer:** That sounds like a good morning. No problem at all. Do you want to grab a coffee first?

**Rasmus:** Got one. Go ahead.

**Interviewer:** Thanks for agreeing to a second slot. After our first chat I noticed I never really got how your team gets from an idea to something live. Could you take me through the last thing you shipped? And at some point, where the developers' agent setup fits in.

**Rasmus:** Okay. One thing first, so you don't overestimate me: I'm not a developer. I'm on the requirements side. The pipeline is the developers' thing. They call it the conveyor belt, half as a joke, and it only runs on the new ordering system. The old one doesn't get anywhere near it.

**Interviewer:** The conveyor belt. Good name. How does work get onto it?

**Rasmus:** Funny enough, the front half didn't change at all. One of us writes the ticket, the whole team chews through it in refinement, and once the room is happy it gets the ready status. The only new thing is a label. Put the label on, the belt takes the ticket. Leave it off, and the ticket just sits there like it always did.

**Interviewer:** Is that label something you've used?

**Rasmus:** Once, as a sort of lesson. A developer grabbed me and a colleague and we did it together on something tiny, I think it was changing the text on a button. The idea was that nobody on the team should find the belt a mystery. So I could do it now. Technically nothing stops me from labelling tickets.

**Interviewer:** And yet.

**Rasmus:** And yet, what would I gain? Say I label ten tickets tomorrow. Every one of them still lands in front of a developer for review before it goes anywhere, and that review queue is already the long one. I'd just make the queue longer. And to be fair, I don't read what the belt builds anyway, that's not my part.

**Interviewer:** Where do you see the result, then?

**Rasmus:** When we accept it. For anything you can see on screen, we go onto the test environment and click around. That's when it becomes real for me.

**Interviewer:** Who's on the team, roughly?

**Rasmus:** Five developers, six on some weeks. One UX colleague. Me. There's also a product owner, but he looks after the whole portfolio, so you see him more around the team than in it. For the new build I'm the one setting the order. Is that project work or product work? Nobody ever decided, and I've stopped asking.

**Interviewer:** Does that bother you?

**Rasmus:** Not really. It just never needed an answer. Anyway.

**Interviewer:** What's a normal week for you?

**Rasmus:** Collecting, mostly. The new system has to hook into things other teams run: stock, delivery slots, payments. And there's a rule that we don't carry anything over just because the old system had it. Which I support. But it means every single behaviour gets argued again. So I go round the teams, I run workshops with them, and then I try to pin down what we actually need.

**Interviewer:** Every behaviour argued again. That's a lot of arguing.

**Rasmus:** It is. That's a whole other conversation, sorry, I'm wandering off.

**Interviewer:** Not at all. Tell me about refinement. You said today was unusual.

**Rasmus:** Yes. I put the tickets in order the day before and drop them into the team chat so people can have a look. This morning we did six, maybe seven, in ninety minutes. That's rare. What we've had a lot lately is one ticket eating thirty minutes and everyone walking out still not agreeing on it.

**Interviewer:** What happens in those thirty minutes?

**Rasmus:** Let me give you the typical case. We do a user test on a Tuesday, three of us are there. On Thursday there's refinement, and someone is at the table who wasn't at the test. What we saw is in three heads, it never really got written down. So the ticket stays thin. And that's how we've ended up building the wrong thing. More than once.

**Interviewer:** Is there an example you can share?

**Rasmus:** I'd rather not pick a specific one. It's still a bit sore. The pattern is always the one I just described.

**Interviewer:** Who's usually the one missing the context? Developers, design, the people who asked for it?

**Rasmus:** Hm. Everyone, at some point. No, wait, that's too easy. It's whoever wasn't in the room that day. It moves around.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing about that, what would it be?

**Rasmus:** One sitting. Developers, design, whoever asked for the thing, all at one table, and the spec gets written right there, together. Instead of a ticket where five people each leave a comment, one after the other, over two weeks. That's the first part.

**Interviewer:** And the second?

**Rasmus:** What the developers should actually get from us at the end. Is it still a ticket? With the belt in there now, I honestly don't know anymore. That's a question I have, not something I've worked out.

**Interviewer:** Thanks, that's useful. Can we switch to you for a bit? Where does AI show up in your own work?

**Rasmus:** Honestly? Whatever's already switched on in the tools we have. It'll draft a slide for me, tidy up a text, fill in a ticket. That's about it. And that's not enough, I know that. I just never block the time to get further.

**Interviewer:** What happens on the days you do try to get further?

**Rasmus:** I get pages back. Pages. And then checking whether all of it is right would take me longer than just writing it, so I close the window and write it myself.

**Interviewer:** And that then goes into refinement the normal way?

**Rasmus:** Yes. Normal way.

**Interviewer:** Back to the belt for a second. What does the team make of it?

**Rasmus:** There's a phrase going around: it's our junior. Which fits, I think. You wouldn't leave a junior alone with anything important, somebody sits next to them. So yes, things get done faster, but nobody's job went away. For now.

**Interviewer:** "For now." Is that said with worry, or with hope?

**Rasmus:** You'd have to ask the developers. How they feel about it, I genuinely don't know.

**Interviewer:** Fair. Last one on refinement: on a good day like today, what's different?

**Rasmus:** I don't know. Maybe the tickets were smaller. Maybe the right people happened to be there. I'd be guessing.

**Interviewer:** That's an honest answer. I think I've got what I came for today.

**Rasmus:** Oh. Really? I was expecting we'd talk more about how I use AI myself. Isn't that what this is about?

**Interviewer:** Not in depth, no. I wanted the team picture first. Your own use is something I'd like to come back to.

**Rasmus:** Okay. To be honest, I wasn't sure what you were after, so I just told you how things are.

**Interviewer:** That's what I hoped for. Thanks, Rasmus.

**Rasmus:** Sure. Then I'll go and click through last sprint's change on the test environment. It's been waiting for me since yesterday.
