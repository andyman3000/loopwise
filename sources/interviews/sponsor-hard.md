# Sponsor interview: hard sponsor

- **Basis:** real conversations, anonymised composite
- **Format:** one-on-one interview, excerpt
- **Date:** Late summer
- **Interviewee:** S, head of product, software company
- **Interviewer:** I

---

**I:** Thanks for the time. Where do things stand on your side?

**S:** What I got approved is a first step. Two days, more of a getting-to-know-each-other thing. Anything beyond that, I need to show the effect. Before I put more budget or more teams on this, I want to talk to someone who has actually delivered it, or see a comparable reference. And internally I need a business case before I even ask.

**I:** Where's the pressure coming from?

**S:** From above. The expectation is roughly: we do the workshop, and the next morning we're twice as efficient. Everyone in the room knows that's not how it works. I like a proper approach, I'm with you on that. But I already know it won't be fast enough for some stakeholders. So I'd want one or two things ready that people can use productively straight after.

If this flops, I look bad in front of my team and in front of the board.

**I:** Have you run trainings before?

**S:** Yes. Booked, everyone thought it was nice, four weeks later everything was as before. It's not rare that we come out of a session, great idea, and then everybody carries on the way they did. We have licences. A chat assistant is rolled out, a few enthusiasts try things, the actual work hasn't changed.

**I:** What would make it different this time?

**S:** When we changed things in engineering, we defined KPIs so we wouldn't lose sight of it afterwards. Are we successful, are we actually using the process we designed? I want the same here. And it will be bumpy at first. People will say it's fine, but we have to push them to actually try it, otherwise we don't get anywhere.

The workshop isn't the hard part. The hard part is holding ourselves accountable afterwards. Meeting every week, a second touchpoint after four or six weeks, going deep again.

**I:** Say we meet four weeks after the two days. How would you know it brought something?

**S:** That's exactly what I'd ask you. What is concretely different four weeks later, and how do we measure it? I'd want to see a change in behaviour, not awareness. People doing something differently on Monday. One or two things that work and are in use from then on, not concepts. And participants carrying it into their own team.

**I:** And how would you measure that?

**S:** Honestly, I haven't worked that out. We haven't done any cost-benefit analysis. And the number of people who'd use a given thing is still so small that I'd want to check again before I say anything. So: how would we know it worked? What would you look at?

**I:** Let me come back to that. What must not happen?

**S:** That people leave thinking this isn't relevant for my work. That it stays a tool course whose clicks are outdated in six months. That the whole thing gets shrunk down to the use-case list of our AI programme. And that the strongest are bored while the weakest drop out on the first morning.

**I:** Tell me about the group.

**S:** It's a range, honestly. From never written a prompt to building their own agents. They're all doing something already, at different levels, and they don't talk to each other. The product owners didn't do the basic agent training we gave the developers.

**I:** And on the tool side? Access?

**S:** That's my other headache. On paper we're equipped. In practice, getting all the access takes longer than building the thing. Last time, the developers all had the rights, and some of the product people didn't, for whatever reason. Right now we can't write from the chat assistant into the ticket system. People would have to create their own API keys for that. Where we're even allowed to store shared skills, I still have to clarify with IT, whether that's an option at all. And not all our teams are set up the same way on the code platform, that needs sorting out beforehand as well.

Compliance-wise, we're standardising on one approved tool. Whatever gets built in the training has to keep living in that tool. I'd rather work with the stack we have than bring in yet another one. And I don't want a free-for-all where people build wild flows and agents nobody needs.

**I:** And after the two days?

**S:** Show me it works. Then we talk about more teams.
