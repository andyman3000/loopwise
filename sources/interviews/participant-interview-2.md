# Participant interview 2

- **Basis:** real conversations, anonymised composite
- **Format:** one-on-one interview, excerpt
- **Date:** Autumn
- **Participant:** P, product owner for a long-running software product that is used at many sites; no line responsibility
- **Interviewer:** I

---

**I:** Thanks for making time. I'd like to go deeper on product work with the developers' agent setup. First, what does product owner mean in your team?

**P:** I have no people reporting to me. I look at which topics the team works on, and whether they bring the most value. I'm less in the detail. I keep other requests away from the team, so they can focus.

**I:** Have you worked with the agent setup yourself?

**P:** No. As far as I understand, it's built around the code. The developers use it once a ticket is refined.

**I:** How does a requirement get to that point today?

**P:** Someone from the business side talks to one of our consultants. The consultant talks to the developers. They write a refined ticket together. Then a developer hands it to the AI. So the AI only comes in at the very end.

**I:** Would you want it earlier?

**P:** That's the question I keep asking. Who is it for? Our product has a few thousand settings. Often nobody knows whether a setting for some function already exists. Today we'd have to ask a developer. I'd like to ask that at the idea stage. Or someone wants a new message window, and the agent says: you already have four windows for similar cases, do you want a fifth?

**I:** What would change if that worked?

**P:** The advice we give would get better. Right now developer time is the bottleneck. In the last few months I drew a lot of architecture diagrams by hand. Then we go to the developers and ask if they're right. I'd like to get the current picture at the push of a button.

**I:** Where does AI show up in your own work today?

**P:** Everywhere, a lot. But that has nothing to do with the developers' setup. Everyone has their own account and builds their own things. I have something that works well for me. Getting it into the team is hard. I'd have to update the wiki and some repository, and keep it current for everyone.

**I:** Could you let the agents write code yourself?

**P:** I could, I have access now. But I don't want to. I wouldn't trust myself with production code from prompts. At home I've built small things. It looked fine, I said go, and it didn't work. Then the AI asked whether it should write tests. I hadn't thought of that. So I'd leave building the setup to the developers. I understand the worry that everyone pushes something out and nothing is right any more.

**I:** How do you make sure a feature helps your goals?

**P:** Since the summer I've been building a goal map. At the top are the few big goals from our product strategy. Under each are smaller goals, and under those the ideas, sorted into now, next and later. Some are marked as things we won't do. Every request of a certain size goes on there.

**I:** How do you measure whether you're getting closer?

**P:** Some goals only show up in a number we get once a year. Others we can see right after a change, in how people use the product. Some features I don't measure at all. A better picture on a screen is part of a smooth experience. I won't claim it moves the usage rate.

**I:** What happens when a request comes in from a site?

**P:** Every ticket has a field where I write the reason, also why we don't do something. When a mail comes in, I give it to the AI and ask it to search the board for the answer. Some questions I've heard five or six times this year. Before, that was an hour per question. Now it's a minute. And half a year later I can still say: we decided that in August, here is why.

**I:** Can you give me an example?

**P:** Last week I sat half a day in a workshop with many people from the sites. Afterwards I spoke my notes into the AI. I told it to comment on every existing ticket that came up. For everything new, it created a ticket with what was said. Half a day of conversation was done in a few minutes.

**I:** Who does discovery in your team?

**P:** Mostly me. Every day.

**I:** And the others?

**P:** That's the part that doesn't work yet. I keep a second brain with my transcripts, mails and slides. I think many people do that. But everyone has their own. What I want is a way we use AI as a team, not each of us alone.

**I:** What could that look like?

**P:** A skill for the goal map that others can use too. It checks whether a request is already there and updates it. But it needs limits. One phone call from a site must not push a ticket into the build. And for me, a weekly summary of what the people with site contact heard.

**I:** Does the developers' agent setup know any of this?

**P:** I don't think so. You'd have to ask a developer. In half a year the goal map will hold a lot of business knowledge. We also have a long strategy document. Maybe that's what the agents should read before they build.

**I:** What else would help the team now?

**P:** I've started asking that we record the refinement. Ten of us talk through ten tickets for an hour and a half. At the end nobody has written anything down. A week later we remember a fraction.

**I:** Thanks, that's our time.

**P:** I liked talking. But what are we trying to achieve with this?

**I:** I'm still working that out with the person who asked me in.
