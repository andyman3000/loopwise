# Participant interview 1

- **Basis:** real conversations, anonymised composite
- **Format:** one-on-one interview, excerpt
- **Date:** Autumn
- **Participant:** R, project and requirements lead in a product team that is rebuilding a customer-facing system; no line responsibility
- **Interviewer:** I

---

**I:** Thanks for taking the time again. Last time I didn't fully understand how your team works. Can you walk me through the last requirement that went live? And where does the developers' agent setup come in?

**R:** Sure. I don't develop, I'm on the requirements side. The developers have built an agent pipeline, they call it the factory. It runs on the new system we're building, not on the old one.

**I:** How does a requirement get in there?

**R:** Same as before, really. A person writes a ticket. The whole team refines it together in the refinement meeting. Then it's marked ready. The factory only picks it up when someone sets a label on the ticket.

**I:** Have you set that label yourself?

**R:** Once. A developer showed me and a colleague how it works, on a small change in the interface. The idea was that everyone understands what the factory does. So I know how it goes now. I could take tickets and set the label.

**I:** But you don't.

**R:** It makes no sense for me. Every change still goes through a review by a developer. That's where the tickets pile up. And I don't look at what the factory produces anyway.

**I:** When do you see the result of your own work?

**R:** At acceptance. When it's something you can see in the interface, we test it on a test environment.

**I:** Who's in the team?

**R:** A handful of developers, a UX colleague, me. And a product owner, but he works more across the whole portfolio. In the new build, I do the prioritising. Whether that's project management or product, we never defined it exactly.

**I:** What does a normal week look like for you?

**R:** A lot of collecting. The new build has to talk to systems in other teams. And nothing gets copied over just because the old system did it that way. So I gather what comes up from the other teams, run workshops, pin the requirements down.

**I:** How does the refinement go?

**R:** I sort the tickets beforehand and send them into the chat. Today we got through six or seven in an hour and a half. That's not normal. Lately we've often spent half an hour on one ticket and still not agreed.

**I:** Why is that?

**R:** Often the knowledge isn't in the room. We come from user tests or a workshop, but someone joins later and doesn't have it. Then the tickets stay thin. More than once we've built the wrong thing.

**I:** What would help?

**R:** Sitting down once with the developers, design and the people asking for it, and writing the spec together. In one session, not in five rounds of comments. And knowing what the handover to the developers should look like. A ticket, or something else.

**I:** Where does AI show up in your own work today?

**R:** Only what's built into the tools. Slides, texts, creating tickets. I think that's too little. I don't spend enough time on it.

**I:** And when you do try more?

**R:** Then I get far too much text back. I'd have to read all of it to know whether it's right. Most of the time I end up writing it myself.

**I:** How does the team see the factory?

**R:** Someone called it a junior developer. It helps build, but never alone. Someone always has to look over its shoulder. So it speeds things up, but it doesn't replace anyone. Not yet, anyway.

**I:** Thanks, that's our time.

**R:** I thought we'd talk more about how I use AI. Was that not the point?

**I:** Not this time. I wanted to understand how the team works first.
