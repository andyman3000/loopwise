# Participant group interview 1

- **Basis:** real conversations, anonymised composite
- **Format:** group interview, excerpt
- **Date:** Spring
- **Participants:** P1, business analyst with product duties · P2, product owner · P3, product manager · P4, project lead, also requirements work
- **Interviewer:** I

---

**I:** Thanks for making the time. There are no wrong answers here, nobody is being tested. I'd like to start with the simple part: where does AI show up in your work today?

**P1:** Every day. Mostly research. I've got the assistant connected to more or less everything we use, the wiki, the team chat, mail. For research that's honestly brilliant. I also use it for stories and wiki pages.

**P4:** For me it's tickets, texts, slides, some analysis, and following up after meetings. The ticket tool is connected.

**I:** Anything that held you back on the access side? Tools, permissions?

**P1:** No. The connectors are all in, that part works for me.

**I:** Okay. Then where does it get stuck?

**P4:** That what comes out isn't what I want. I'm trying to build a view for project management, are we on plan or not, and the result just doesn't look the way I picture it. In a spreadsheet I could show that better. So my real question is: where do I actually put the assistant so I get a result that helps me?

**P1:** Same feeling from a different angle. I'm fairly sure I'm not getting the full potential out of it in my daily work. But I couldn't tell you where. It's honestly a hunch. There has to be more.

**I:** What would help you find out where?

**P1:** Maybe I'd see it if I saw good examples. That might do it.

**I:** P4, you mentioned a training earlier. What did you take from it?

**P4:** I only went to part of it. And it didn't do that much for me, to be honest. The description had already put me off a bit, it was written very much for developers. In the session we mostly typed in prompts that were already prepared and waited for things to run through. I got an understanding of how it's put together. But not in a way that helped me personally.

**P2:** That's the pattern I know from most workshops. On the day everything is great, and then the next morning you carry on exactly the way you did before.

**I:** Was that the case for you this time?

**P2:** This time, no. It was practical. It was what I'd hoped for, I'm actually taking something away I can use. Usually that's the hard part, getting it into the working day. This time I have a good feeling about it.

**I:** So the test would be that for the next task you open the assistant instead of doing it on your own?

**P2:** Exactly. And that's a good feeling.

**I:** P3, what were you hoping for going in?

**P3:** That I'd know how to use AI concretely as a product manager, to move my product forward. Practical examples I can carry straight over into my day. I didn't want to walk out with knowledge only, I wanted approaches I can actually try.

**I:** How would you notice that?

**P3:** Three weeks later a colleague asks me what I'm doing differently, and I have an answer. And at least one thing from the format is in my daily work.

**I:** P4, when you do get a good result, what happens with it?

**P4:** Mostly nothing. I keep having things created from scratch. When I need something similar again, I don't reuse what I had. I just let it create it again.

**I:** Let me ask about the group itself. You all have quite similar titles. Did the level in the room feel similar?

**P1:** Not at all. It's a very mixed field with us. By definition we all have the same job title. What people actually do day to day is quite different.

**I:** One last question. If you think of the next few weeks, what would you want?

**P1:** For me a conversation like this one is fine, to be honest. But if it comes to a training, I'd be very glad.

**P4:** Something where I'm not worried that I stop thinking about the solution myself. It's faster, sure. But I do worry that I just type everything in, get the finished answer, glance at it and send it off.

**I:** Thank you, that's very helpful.
