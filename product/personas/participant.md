## Mixed level, same title

In the three group interviews, two of 13 participants say they already run agents
(`interviews/participants-group-2.md:17`, `interviews/participants-group-3.md:15`), three describe
themselves as beginners or not advanced (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:15`,
`interviews/participants-group-2.md:51`, `interviews/participants-group-3.md:13`). Sponsors see the
same spread (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:45`, `emails/inquiry-06.md:14`).

## Goals

- Practical examples to carry straight into the day, not knowledge only (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:47`).
- Apply it to features actually on the roadmap (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:43`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:41`).
- A plan for the next weeks with next steps (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:43`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:42`).
- Someone who sits with them on their own topic (`interviews/participants-group-3.md:59`).
- Material to reread afterwards (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:57`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:56`).
- A shared way of working and sharing skills in the team (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:55`, `interviews/participants-group-3.md:63`).

## Frustrations

- **Back to normal the next morning** (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:35`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:45`).
- **Does not know where it helps.** "It's honestly a hunch. There has to be more." (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:25`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:37`)
- **Format built for someone else:** written for developers, prepared prompts (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:33`); tools the company does not allow (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:25`).
- **Lost at the start:** no schedule, no goal for the day (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:47`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:52`); following a screen remotely while working (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:21`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:53`).
- **Setup friction:** laptop memory (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:29`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:58`), no admin rights (`inbox/inbox-export.md:77`).
- **Time** to find out where it went wrong (`interviews/participants-group-3.md:23`).

## Counter-evidence to keep in view

- Access is not a blocker for several: `interviews/participants-group-1.md:19`, `interviews/participants-group-3.md:47`, `inbox/inbox-export.md:65`.
- Some do take it into their work: `interviews/participants-group-1.md:39`, `interviews/participants-group-3.md:25`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:46`.

## How they decide

They do not book. Their own test is whether they open the assistant for the next task
(`interviews/participants-group-1.md:41`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:45`). What they tell the
sponsor afterwards is what the sponsor hears (`interviews/sponsor-soft.md:25`).

## How they arrive

Lines from the synthetic follow-up questions (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:73`, `interviews/participants-group-3.md:71`).

- Invited by their lead with a date and a link, nothing more (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:86`, `interviews/participants-group-1.md:88`).
- Some volunteer, others are put on the list (`interviews/participants-group-3.md:82`, `interviews/participants-group-3.md:84`).
- Want to know beforehand what the day looks like and whether it is written for developers again (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:98`).
- Want to know whether to bring a task of their own (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:100`, `inbox/inbox-export.md:29`).

## On the website

- Opens the link the evening before and sees several formats. Cannot tell which one was booked, and finds nothing on what to prepare (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:92`).
- Others never open the link and wait for the trainer (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:94`).

## What they say

- "On the day everything is great, and then the next morning you carry on exactly the way you did before." (`interviews/participants-group-1.md:35`)
- "I'll have an idea of what I could do with it. Not: this is what I'm going to do with it." (`interviews/participants-group-2.md:13`)
- "Honestly, someone who sits with me on my own topic." (`interviews/participants-group-3.md:59`)

## What the participant is NOT

Not the buyer (`interviews/sponsor-soft.md:71`, `interviews/sponsor-hard.md:13`). Not one level. Not a developer by default (`interviews/sponsor-soft.md:17`).

## What we do not know yet

- Whether bringing their own task changes what they report four weeks later. No follow-up data yet.
- The level spread in a typical booked group. The interviews give 13 self-descriptions, not a distribution.

## Added from two one-on-one interviews (2026-11-12)

**Basis:** two one-on-one interviews, synthetic, built from patterns in real conversations: `sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md` and `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md`. Both participants work at the same grocery retailer, in different product teams (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:6`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:6`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:29`). Both are on the requirements side and have no line responsibility (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:6`, `sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:19`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:6`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:21`). The lines above stay as they were.

### How far each one got with AI

- One participant uses only the AI built into the office tools. The participant calls that too little (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:79`).
- When this participant tries more, the AI returns too much text to check. The participant then writes it alone (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:83`).
- The other participant uses AI for almost everything (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:53`).
- This participant answers recurring questions with AI in a minute instead of an hour (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:77`).
- This participant also turns half a day of workshop notes into tickets in minutes (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:81`).

### Each person works with AI alone

- Everyone has their own account and builds their own helpers (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:57`).
- Sharing a working helper with the team is hard, because someone has to keep it current (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:57`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:61`).
- Everyone keeps their own notes for the AI. The participant wants the team to use AI together (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:93`).

### The developers' agent setup stops at the developers

- One participant was shown the setup once. Using it makes no sense to the participant, because every change waits for a developer review (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:27`, `sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:31`).
- The other participant has never used it. AI only comes in once a developer has a refined ticket (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:29`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:33`).
- This participant wants to ask the AI earlier, at the idea stage (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:37`).
- This participant thinks the setup does not know the business goals (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:101`).

### Trouble before the build

- The team often spends half an hour on one ticket and does not agree (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:55`).
- People join without the knowledge from user tests. Tickets stay thin, and the team has built the wrong thing (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:59`).
- The participant wants to write the spec once, together with developers, design and the people asking (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:71`).
- In this participant's own team, ten people go through ten tickets in one refinement and nobody writes anything down (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:105`).

### Counter-evidence to keep in view

- One participant does use AI often and saves time with it (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:53`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:77`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:81`).
- This participant chooses not to let AI write production code. The reason is a failed attempt without tests, not missing access (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:65`).

### What we do not know yet

- Both interviews end with the participant unsure what the talk was for (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:103`, `sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:107`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:109`). What they said may follow what they thought the interviewer wanted.
- [assumption: both participants were picked because they are open to AI. Participants who doubt AI are missing from the one-on-one interviews.]
- Two interviews describe two people, not a spread across a booked group.
