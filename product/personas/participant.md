## Mixed level, same title

In the three one-on-one interviews, one participant uses AI for most of the work
(`interviews/participant-jannik.md:53`), one uses only the AI built into the tools
(`interviews/participant-rasmus.md:79`), and one had hardly opened the chat tool
(`interviews/participant-francoise.md:37`). In one workshop's opening round, the range went from an
architect with his own agent routine to people who had never worked with any of this
(`interviews/participant-francoise.md:41`, `interviews/participant-francoise.md:45`). Sponsors see the
same spread (`interviews/sponsor-thomas.md:121`, `interviews/sponsor-sabine.md:69`, `emails/inquiry-06.md:14`).

## Goals

- Something practical that takes repetitive work off them (`interviews/participant-francoise.md:33`).
- Apply it to features actually on the roadmap (`feedback/feedback-export.md:41`).
- A plan for the next weeks with next steps (`feedback/feedback-export.md:42`).
- Someone who sits with them on their own problem (`interviews/participant-francoise.md:177`).
- Material to reread afterwards (`feedback/feedback-export.md:56`).
- A shared way of working and sharing helpers in the team (`interviews/participant-jannik.md:93`, `interviews/participant-francoise.md:157`, `interviews/participant-francoise.md:169`).

## Frustrations

- **Back to normal the next morning** (`feedback/feedback-export.md:45`).
- **Does not know where it helps.** "I could not really place yet where this fits" (`feedback/feedback-export.md:38`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:37`)
- **Format built for someone else:** on a technical day a participant copied the screen "with no idea what any of it did" (`interviews/participant-francoise.md:101`); company AI offers aimed at developers (`interviews/sponsor-carla.md:93`); only one of the trainer's tools cleared (`interviews/participant-francoise.md:37`).
- **Lost at the start:** no schedule, no goal for the day (`feedback/feedback-export.md:52`); following a screen remotely while working (`feedback/feedback-export.md:53`).
- **Setup friction:** laptop memory (`feedback/feedback-export.md:58`), no admin rights (`inbox/inbox-export.md:77`), an install route through the command line (`interviews/sponsor-sabine.md:89`).
- **Time** to check what the AI returns: checking takes longer than writing it (`interviews/participant-rasmus.md:83`).

## Counter-evidence to keep in view

- Access is not a blocker for several: `interviews/participant-jannik.md:65`, `interviews/sponsor-thomas.md:113`, `inbox/inbox-export.md:65`.
- Some do take it into their work: `interviews/participant-jannik.md:77`, `interviews/participant-jannik.md:81`, `feedback/feedback-export.md:46`.

## How they decide

They do not book. Their own test is whether they open the assistant for the next task
(`feedback/feedback-export.md:45`). What they tell the sponsor afterwards is what the sponsor hears
(`interviews/sponsor-sabine.md:185`).

## How they arrive

From the one-on-one interviews and the inbox.

- Get a calendar invite they did not ask for (`interviews/participant-francoise.md:29`).
- Some ask to join, others are put on the list (`interviews/sponsor-thomas.md:17`, `interviews/sponsor-thomas.md:21`, `interviews/participant-francoise.md:29`).

## Added from two one-on-one interviews (2026-11-12)

**Basis:** two one-on-one interviews, synthetic, built from patterns in real conversations: `sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md` and `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md`. Both participants work at the same grocery retailer, in different product teams (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:6`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:6`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:29`). Both are on the requirements side and have no line responsibility (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:6`, `sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:19`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:6`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:21`).

### How far each one got with AI

- One participant uses only the AI built into the office tools. The participant calls that too little (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:79`).
- When this participant tries more, the AI returns too much text to check. The participant then writes it alone (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:83`).
- The other participant uses AI for almost everything (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:53`).
- This participant answers recurring questions with AI in a minute instead of an hour (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:77`).
- This participant also turns half a day of workshop notes into tickets in minutes (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:81`).

### Each person works with AI alone

- Everyone has their own account and builds their own helpers (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:57`).
- Sharing a working helper with the team is hard, because someone has to keep it current (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:57`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:61`).
- Everyone keeps their own notes for the AI. The participant wants the team to use AI together (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:93`).

### The developers' agent setup stops at the developers

- One participant was shown the setup once. Using it makes no sense to the participant, because every change waits for a developer review (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:27`, `sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:31`).
- The other participant has never used it. AI only comes in once a developer has a refined ticket (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:29`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:33`).
- This participant wants to ask the AI earlier, at the idea stage (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:37`).
- This participant thinks the setup does not know the business goals (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:101`).

### Trouble before the build

- The team often spends half an hour on one ticket and does not agree (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:55`).
- People join without the knowledge from user tests. Tickets stay thin, and the team has built the wrong thing (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:59`).
- The participant wants to write the spec once, together with developers, design and the people asking (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:71`).
- In this participant's own team, ten people go through ten tickets in one refinement and nobody writes anything down (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:105`).

### Counter-evidence to keep in view

- One participant does use AI often and saves time with it (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:53`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:77`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:81`).
- This participant chooses not to let AI write production code. The reason is a failed attempt without tests, not missing access (`sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:65`).

### What we do not know yet

- Both interviews end with the participant unsure what the talk was for (`sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:103`, `sources/interviews/participant-rasmus.md:107`, `sources/interviews/participant-jannik.md:109`). What they said may follow what they thought the interviewer wanted.
- [assumption: both participants were picked because they are open to AI. Participants who doubt AI are missing from the one-on-one interviews.]
- Two interviews describe two people, not a spread across a booked group.
