## Context

- Two days approved, "more of a getting-to-know-each-other thing" (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:13`).
- Pressure from above: the next morning twice as efficient, which everyone knows is not how it works (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:17`).
- Licences rolled out, a chat assistant in place, "the actual work hasn't changed" (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:23`).
- The group ranges from never written a prompt to building their own agents; product owners missed the agent training developers got (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:45`).
- Access gaps: product people lacked rights developers had, no write access from chat to tickets, shared skill storage unclear with IT (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:49`).
- Standardising on one approved tool; what gets built must keep living there (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:51`).

## Goals

- Show the effect, then get more budget and more teams (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:13`, `:55`).
- One or two things ready that people can use productively straight after (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:17`).
- Behaviour change, not awareness; participants carry it into their own team (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:33`).
- KPIs defined up front, as they did for an engineering change (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:27`).
- Accountability afterwards: weekly, and a second touchpoint after four or six weeks (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:29`).

## Frustrations

- **Trainings that faded.** "four weeks later everything was as before" (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:23`).
- **Personal risk.** "If this flops, I look bad in front of my team and in front of the board." (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:19`)
- **Wrong kind of format.** A tool course that dates, a use-case list, the strongest bored and the weakest out on the first morning (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:41`).
- **Access overhead.** "getting all the access takes longer than building the thing" (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:49`).
- **Sprawl.** No "free-for-all" of flows and agents nobody needs (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:51`).

## How they decide

- Wants to talk to someone who delivered it, or a comparable reference (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:13`).
- Needs an internal business case (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:13`).
- Demands a measure but has none: no cost-benefit analysis, and asks the provider what to look at (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:37`).
- Next step depends on proof: "Show me it works. Then we talk about more teams." (`interviews/sponsor-hard.md:55`)
